How to use Timeline Visualizer

From a Google Maps Timeline export to a finished video, in about ten minutes.

On this page

  4. Select a period
  5. Set the title and look
  6. Create the video
  7. Watch, share, and manage

1. Export your Timeline file

Timeline Visualizer never touches your Google account. You export the data yourself, and the app reads only the file you hand it.

On Android

  1. Open Settings on the phone (not the Google Maps app).
  2. Go to Location → Location services → Timeline.
  3. Select Export Timeline data.
  4. Save the file — it is usually called Timeline.json — somewhere easy to find, such as Downloads.

Labels vary a little between phone makers and Android versions. The app's Get Timeline file button opens Location settings for you, because Android offers no direct link to the Timeline page.

On iPhone or iPad

  1. Open Google Maps.
  2. Tap your profile picture, then Settings.
  3. Go to Personal content → Export Timeline data and save the file.

Use the phone export, not Google Takeout. Older Takeout archives use a different format that the app cannot read. The export has to come from the Timeline screen on a phone.

2. If older trips are missing

After changing phones, reinstalling Google Maps, or resetting a device, your Timeline may only contain recent journeys. If Timeline backup was enabled on the old device, you can import that backup in Google Maps first:

  1. Open Google Maps and sign in with the account used on the previous device.
  2. Tap your profile picture, then Your Timeline.
  3. Tap the cloud or backup icon in the upper-right corner.
  4. Under Your backups, choose the device holding the older Timeline.
  5. Open More, select Import, and confirm.
  6. Check that the older visits are visible, then export a fresh Timeline file as above.

Restoring a backup and exporting a JSON file are separate steps — importing in Google Maps does not send anything to Timeline Visualizer. The app cannot access, unlock, or repair a Google backup. If no backup was ever created, deleted Timeline data cannot be recovered.

3. Choose the file in the app

  1. Open Timeline Visualizer and tap New video in the bottom bar.
  2. Under Timeline file, tap Choose file.
  3. Select the Timeline.json you saved.

Before your first import, the app explains that drawing the map sends tile coordinates to CARTO and lets you cancel. Reading a large export takes a moment — you will see Reading Timeline file and then Preparing journey.

The chosen file is remembered, so the next video starts straight at the period picker.

4. Select a period

Two ways to choose what the video covers:

  • Month range. Set a start year and month and an end year and month. The range is inclusive and may cross years.
  • Exact dates. Turn on Choose exact dates and pick a start and end date.

Under the selection, the app shows the number of location points, the approximate distance in kilometres, and the dates covered. If it reports no points, no movement, or a single point, widen the period.

The preview animates the selection right away. Use Preview and Pause preview, or drag the position bar to jump through the journey.

5. Set the title and look

  • Video title template. Use {year} and {name} — for example {year} {name}'s Timeline. Fill in Name in automatic title once and it is reused.
  • Route animation length. A preset, or Custom… for any whole number from 10 to 300 seconds. Longer videos take longer to render and use more storage.
  • Map style and perspective. Light or dark, and Flat, 3D, or 3D + terrain.
  • Route line and marker. Pick a preset colour or a custom one by hue, shade, or hex.
  • Orientation. Square for feeds, portrait for stories, landscape for a screen.
  • Advanced settings. Camera movement, route context, local framing, zoom-in smoothness, long-hop sensitivity, long-trip compression, and video quality. Restore defaults undoes all of it.

Anything set in Settings becomes the default for the next preview and export, so you only tune it once.

6. Create the video

  1. Tap Create video.
  2. The app prepares the map, renders the frames, and finishes the file — progress is shown throughout with an estimated time remaining.
  3. On Android, allow notifications if you want progress and a completion alert while the app is in the background. Declining does not stop the export.
  4. When it finishes you can Watch video, share it, save a copy elsewhere, or Create another video.

Cancel video creation stops the export at any point. On Android the finished MP4 is saved to Movies/Timeline Visualizer; on iPhone it is added to your photo library.

On iPhone, keep the app in the foreground for long exports — iOS only grants a few minutes of background time.

7. Watch, share, and manage your videos

  • My videos lists everything you have created, with a thumbnail, title, date, length, and the period it covers.
  • Add videos brings existing MP4 files into the same list.
  • Journey overview gives you a 1080 × 1080 image of the complete route to save or share.
  • Save as writes a copy of a video to a location you choose. Very large files open the share sheet instead.
  • Remove from list only removes the entry; Delete video permanently deletes the MP4 and asks first. Deleting a whole library is a separate, confirmed action.

Tips for a better video

  • Start with a year. A twelve-month range at 30–60 seconds is a good first video; refine from there.
  • Match the camera to the trip. Fixed suits a single city, Dynamic suits long-distance travel where detail matters.
  • Use compression for flights. If one long leg dominates, set long-trip compression to Balanced or Strong so shorter trips still show.
  • Preview at any quality. The preview is the same renderer, so check framing before exporting at 1080p.
  • Plan for storage and time. Higher quality and longer animations mean longer renders and larger files.
  • Try terrain on mountain trips. 3D + terrain adds real ground heights, which suits hikes and road trips through hills.

Troubleshooting

What you seeWhat to do
This file is not valid or complete JSON The export was interrupted or the wrong file was chosen. Export again and pick the saved Timeline.json.
This is an older Google Takeout format Export Timeline data from the phone's Timeline screen instead of using a Takeout archive.
This export contains raw signals but no reconstructed Timeline journeys Google has not built journeys from the raw data yet. Check that Timeline is on in Google Maps, wait, and export again.
This Timeline export contains no location data Timeline was probably off during that period, so Google had nothing to export.
The previously used Timeline is no longer available The file was moved or deleted. Choose the Timeline.json file again.
No location points in this period Widen the date or month range, or check that the export covers those dates.
Could not create video Free up storage and try a shorter length or lower quality, then create the video again.
This video could not be played inside Timeline Visualizer Use Open in another app to play it with your usual video player.
Suspicious locations ignored Expected. Isolated, impossible GPS points were skipped; turn filtering off in settings if you want them drawn.
